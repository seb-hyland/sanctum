# Clinical Problem
- Many (estimated 80%) of patients suffer from needle phobia
- Other delivery methods (e.g., oral) have issues such as degradation in the GI tract, first-pass hepatic metabolism, etc.
- Delivery via buccal mucosa would improve both patient experiences and efficacy

## [!def] mucosa

# Research Gap
- In order to ensure optimal efficiency, the delivery formation must maintain contact with the buccal mucosa for an extended period
- It is difficult to do so, due to flushes of saliva, swallowing, and mouth/muscle movement
- THUS, it is necessary to achieve strong adhesion under the wet conditions of the oral cavity
- CURRENTLY, few formulations exhibit excellent properties to overcome these limitations

1. Describe the experimental groups used in the study. What are each of their functions in the experimental design?

No treatment:
- This serves as a negative control group
- Provides a reference of natural bodily healing (wound closure over time)

Kanghua Dex Film
- Provides a baseline comparison against current commercially-available solutions

PVA@PLGA-PDA-Dex, PVA-DOPA6@PLGA-Dex, PVA-DOPA6@PLGA-PDA-Dex

2. Summarize the major results of the experiment
- In this figure, the degree of wound closure under each experimental group is presented after 2, 5, and 8 days
- In all days, the "no treatment" group performed the worst, followed by the Kanghua group, the PVA@PLGA-PDA-Dex group, the PVA-DOPA6@PLGA-Dex group, and then PVA-DOPA6@PLGA-PDA-Dex group
- After two days, there is a small difference in mean degree of wound closure across experimental groups
- The differences increase after 5 and 8 days, with a statistically significant difference between the best (PVA-DOPA6@PLGA-PDA-Dex) group and the runner-up (PVA-DOPA6@PLGA-Dex) after 5 days
- On day 5, all novel formulations except PVA@PLGA-PDA-Dex outperformed Kanghua Dex Film in a statistically significant manner; on day 8, this was true for ALL novel formulations
